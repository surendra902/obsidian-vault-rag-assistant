# Zettelkasten

The Zettelkasten is Niklas Luhmann's slip-box method: a web of small, self-contained notes whose value comes from the links between them, not from any single note. Luhmann produced 70 books with it, which is the usual selling point.

## The three kinds of notes

1. **Fleeting notes** — throwaway captures. Whatever is in your head right now, written down in seconds, to be processed or deleted within a day or two.
2. **Literature notes** — what a source said, in your own words, with the reference. One per source (or per idea from a source).
3. **Permanent notes** — exactly one idea per note, written as if for a stranger, densely linked to other permanent notes. These are the output.

The pipeline is fleeting → literature → permanent. Anything that never becomes a permanent note was either not important or not understood yet.

## Rules I follow

- **Atomicity**: one note, one idea. If a note needs an "and" in its title, it's two notes. ([[how-to-take-smart-notes]] is the book that convinced me.)
- **Write in full sentences**, not keywords. Six months later a keyword is noise; a sentence is a thought.
- **Link before filing**: the folder location matters less than the links. This is why backlinks, see [[wikilinks-and-backlinks]], do more work than folders.
- No orphan notes — a permanent note with no links is a dead end and I treat it as a bug.

## Where it meets the rest of the system

Fleeting notes land in the same inbox I process during the [[weekly-review]]. The permanent notes live in one flat folder — [[vault-organization]] explains why folders stop mattering once linking is habitual.

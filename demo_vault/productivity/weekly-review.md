# Weekly Review

The one productivity ritual that survived every system change. Friday afternoon, 30 minutes, four steps in order:

1. **Empty the inboxes** — note inbox, email, downloads folder. Everything gets filed into [[para-method|PARA]] buckets, done, or deleted. Nothing is "left for later"; later is next week's review.
2. **Audit projects** — for each active project: does it still have a next action? No next action means either pick one now or admit the project is dead and archive it.
3. **Look at the calendar, both directions** — last week for anything unfollowed-up, next two weeks for anything that needs prep this weekend.
4. **Pick next week's priorities** — three, maximum. More than three is a wish list.

## Why it works

Every capture system — GTD's inbox, [[zettelkasten]]'s fleeting notes, PARA's buckets — is a deferred-decision queue. Deferral only pays if the decisions actually happen on schedule. The review is that schedule; skip two weeks and the queues stop being trusted, which is the moment the whole system collapses into "I'll just remember it."

## Failure modes I've hit

- **Doing it Monday morning** — it becomes triage of already-missed things instead of planning. Friday works because the cost of a missed item is one weekend.
- **Skipping when busy** — exactly backwards; busy weeks are when unfollowed commitments pile up invisibly.
- **Turning it into a work session** — 30 minutes of reviewing, zero minutes of doing. Filing a thing is not doing the thing.

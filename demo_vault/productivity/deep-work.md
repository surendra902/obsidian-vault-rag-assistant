# Deep Work

Cal Newport's term for sustained, undistracted concentration on a cognitively demanding task — and his claim that it's both increasingly rare and increasingly valuable exactly because it's rare.

## The practical core

- **Depth requires blocks, not moments.** Real concentration ramps up over 15–20 minutes; context-switching resets it. A day of 25-minute slots is shallow by construction. The useful unit is a **2–4 hour block** with no inputs.
- **Attention residue**: switching tasks leaves part of your cognition stuck on the previous task. Even a "quick check" of messages costs the next twenty minutes of quality.
- **Boredom tolerance is trainable** — and the training is the point. Every impulse to check the phone during a dull stretch is a rep. Give in habitually and deep blocks become physically uncomfortable.

## Routines that produce it

Scheduled blocks (same slot daily, defended like a meeting), startup rituals (same desk, same playlist, same first action), and hard shutdowns at day's end so unfinished work doesn't leak into recovery time.

## Where it meets the rest of the system

Deep blocks are for the hard thing ([[project-rag-assistant]]'s indexer rewrite qualifies); everything else — inbox processing, filing, quick replies — is shallow work and belongs batched outside them. The [[weekly-review]] decides what deserves next week's blocks; the blocks are the only place Projects actually move.

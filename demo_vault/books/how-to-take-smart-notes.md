# How to Take Smart Notes

Sönke Ahrens on Niklas Luhmann's [[zettelkasten]]. The book's argument: writing is not the record of thinking — it **is** the thinking. A note you can't rewrite into a sentence isn't understood yet.

## The method restated

Fleeting notes capture in the moment; literature notes record what a source said in your own words; permanent notes — one idea each, written for a stranger, densely linked — are the only real output. The slip-box is the collection of permanent notes, and it, not the individual notes, is the thinking tool.

## The claims that changed how I work

- **The note is the unit of work**, not the book or the project. "I read X" is nothing; "here is the idea I took from X and here is what it contradicts" is a deposit you can spend.
- **Forces elaboration**: translating a source into your own words is a comprehension test with no way to cheat. Copying quotes passes no such test.
- **Interest-driven, not schedule-driven**: Luhmann worked on whatever the slip-box surfaced as connected to his current questions. The links *are* the research agenda.
- **The slip-box compounds**: notes gain value per new link, so an old note is an asset, not a cost — the opposite of a folder of stale docs.

## Honest limits

Curation bias: a slip-box full of mutually agreeing notes becomes an echo chamber; the habit needs deliberate contradictory notes. And the method pays off on a delay — months before the links start generating questions you didn't plant. Same patience curve as [[evaluating-rag-systems|running an eval before tuning]]: trust the process, measure later.

# Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman, 2011. Forty years of cognitive-psychology research organized around one distinction: the mind runs two systems.

- **System 1** — fast, automatic, associative, always on. It answers "2+2" and "is that face angry?" without effort, and it answers *something* even when it doesn't know.
- **System 2** — slow, deliberate, effortful, lazy. It computes 17×24 and checks logic, but only when recruited, and it tires quickly.

## The findings that stuck

- **Substitution**: when a hard question ("should I take this job?") meets a lazy System 2, System 1 silently answers an easier one ("do I like this interviewer?") — and we report the answer as if it were to the original question.
- **Anchoring**: irrelevant numbers drag estimates toward themselves. Seen in negotiation, pricing, and any estimate made after hearing a figure.
- **WYSIATI** — *what you see is all there is*: we build coherent stories from whatever evidence is present and feel no lack for what's missing. Confidence tracks coherence of the story, not quality of the evidence.

## Why it's in this vault

WYSIATI is the psychology of [[hallucination-and-grounding]]: a model with incomplete context produces a fluent, coherent answer and no sense of the gap. The grounding machinery — retrieval thresholds, citations, refusal — is a WYSIATI patch. And substitution is the argument for [[evaluating-rag-systems|measuring instead of vibing]]: asked "is this system good?", everyone — including the builder — answers "did the last three answers look good?"

**Caveat worth keeping**: several priming studies behind the book failed replication. The core two-system framing and the heuristics-and-biases program held up far better than the flashier social-priming chapters.
